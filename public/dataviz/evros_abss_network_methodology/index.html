Automatic Surveillance Network

The Evros border has been a testing ground and a funnel for a host of EU-wide research into surveillance and machine vision which involves a wide arrange of stakeholders across the EU. A lot of this is showcased during the Greek Ministry of Migration’s presentation of the REACTION program.
January 25, 2025

Evros ABSS#

Summary#

The Evros Automated Border Surveillance System (ABSS) is a permanent, fixed-location layer of the border security apparatus. Its coverage ranged from the river Delta, all the way to the north tri-border near the city of Orestiada. I geolocated the nodes of this network through satellite imagery, public tenders, and various press articles and photographs.

Detailed Methodology#

The Evros border has been a testing ground and a funnel for a host of EU-wide research (CERETAB, AIDERS και ROBORDER and projects employing those) into surveillance and machine vision which involves a wide arrange of stakeholders from across different law enforcement and research centers in the EU. A lot of this is showcased during the Greek Ministry of Migration’s presentation of the REACTION program. The autonomous surveillance systems presented show their applications in urban environments with identification of people and vehicles and “total monitoring” of set areas. They are created to be deployed in ad-hoc scenarios by staff on the ground.

ABSS Tender#

The Automated Border Surveillance System (ABSS) is a permanent, fixed-location additional layer of the border security apparatus alongside the fence, the increasingly militarized (including new military-issue patrol vehicles up to deploying confiscated MRAPs for patrol purposes) and technologically upgraded police/ border guard patrols, and various other actions (including the expansion of the anti-armor trench between the two countries alongside increased army presence, to the militias being formed by locals and donations of thermal cameras to army units, heartbeat sensors at checkpoints, etc).

The projects presented and listed are sponsored through a combination of EU and GR funding. The EU funding for research is from the Horizon2020 as well as case by case border reinforcement programs. The funding split ranged from 75% to 90% of EU funds.

In 2016, the Hellenic Police initiates a public tender for “Expansion of the automated border surveillance system in the river area of the Hellenic-Turkish border at the Evros area and the interconnection of the Regional Centers for Integrated Border Management and Migration (RCIBMM (PEKODISME in Greek))” The 14.9 million euros were 75% funded by the Internal Security Fund (ISF) of the Directorate-General for Migration and Home Affairs of the European Commission (DG HOME).

The project was undertaken by Space Hellas S.A. (GR) and OTE S.A. (GR).

On the 20th of October 2021, mainstream media in Greece reported that the system, which they dubbed the “electronic shield of Evros” had started its trial phase, and would be in full operation a month later in November. The system is reported to cover the Evros border completely, from the mouth of the river delta, all the way up to the village of Sofiko, close to the city of Orestiada.

Mainstream reporting shows the pylons being put up as well as some imagery of the local centers / camera control rooms.

Open channel

The pylons where the system was built on, were largely purpose-built. With some already existing infrastructure, mostly belonging to OTE S.A., were used especially for parts of the network that would function as relays or local control centers (without camera equipment). Due to the pylons being owned and administered by the Ministry of Citizen Protection they were exempted from being registered on the directory of high-powered communications antennae, where the rest of the pylons and antennae can be found. Owing to them also being classified as national security assets they were additionally exempt from any natural environment laws and protections (for example being built deep into Natura2000 areas).

Antennae information in Greece is publicly available here where one can search for them by municipality/general area or by map pin.

Although the locations were not specified, either due to how early in the process this tender was drafted or due to security reasons, they create an initial impression of how the network is structured. Further in the document we find more information regarding the structure. This gives some insight into the trunk and branches. Much of the specification is there for the purpose of showing a basic structure and intent with further elaboration and details during the final design phase after the contract has been awarded.

Mapping the network#

The diagram above was the starting point to create the network map. I already knew the location of the Alexandroupolis and Orestiada Regional centers.

According to the specifications as well as published material we knew that the local centers were to be found in the Border Guard/Police stations in the smaller towns.


Along the, now known, centers/nodes of the network, the tender’s provisional planning also included specification (as distance between each in meters) for the wireless high-powered coupling between the antennae themselves and then to the primary/trunk network.

Through these point to point distances between the nodes, some of them were already known which allowed for verification of the distances. At the same time it showed that the specified distances were not completely accurate. Probably due to deviations during the final planning and further changes during the construction phase.

Some minor deviations in distance, especially in cases where the building complexes were spaced far enough apart, were ignored. (The Didymoteicho police academy and the Feres/Poros border guard stations are examples of this). There has also been reporting that places the Regional Center of Alexandroupolis at the city’s airport, this was also ignored in favor of the specification that sees the receivers placed on top of the Police Directorate’s building.

Based on this, I began identifying and placing these points on the composite map of Evros I created. (The network line at the southern edge, points towards the Police Headquarters in Athens were image is simultaneously broadcasted.)

The placement employed a variety of OSINT methods (like social media posts by activists, mainstream media, government announcements and photographs, satellite imagery).

Determination of range#

Another factor that assisted in the placement of the pylons beyond the distance between known points, the elevation, and the corresponding local and regional center, was the range of equipment mounted on those platforms.

The initial tender specifies for each, a thermal camera, a surface radar, and a laser rangefinder.

This factor defined any disputed location because the range of the thermal camera had to be able to reach the river and the border. (and in many cases penetrate quite far in both the Turkish and the Greek mainland)

The next step was to match and visualize the maximum range that the pylons would be able to monitor and look for humans.

This resulted in the following:

Showcasing that since its trial run in 2021 the system could provide nearly total coverage of the river Evros and the border area, sometimes stretching far inland. Due to the morphology of the river floodplain and the elevated position of the surveillance equipment it can be inferred that the thermal cameras can have a view of human activity in the area. This is also supplemented by the automatic detection software also specified.

Furthermore the equipment was specified to have a very high degree of freedom covering both horizontal and vertical angles.

These are the thermal cameras and radar on the pylons. They sometimes vary in placement where they might be hanging on the side of the pylon (and thus have less horizontal rotational freedom).

Smaller Scale Surveillance Equipment#

Manned guard posts along the river (from the second phase of the river fence expansion) as well as the army posts are equipped with cameras and thermal cameras in order to assist with the monitoring.

These while providing information to law enforcement are not interconnected with the larger ABSS network.

In addition to the guard posts, the patrol network is providing another layer, this time of human, surveillance which is further assisted by drones operating on an ad-hoc basis.

This is also a focus of the research being conducted through the programs detailed at the start of the document.
