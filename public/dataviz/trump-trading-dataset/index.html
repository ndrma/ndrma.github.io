Language and Framing Guide for Your Piece#

Do use:

  • “BBC investigation identified”
  • “Reported pattern of unusual trading activity”
  • “Bets placed [X] minutes before announcement”
  • “Alleged anomaly”
  • “The BBC reported that…”
  • “Analysts quoted by the BBC called the activity ‘abnormal’”

Don’t use:

  • “Insider trading” as an assertion (it’s an allegation, no prosecutions)
  • “Trump’s team” or “administration officials traded” (not proven)
  • “Leak” as assertion (in the headline of the BBC piece it’s a question, not a claim)

For every number on the interactive, include attribution. The tooltip or detail view for each incident should show the primary source. “Source: BBC investigation, April 2026” at minimum, with secondary sources where relevant.

What This Dataset Does and Doesn’t Do#

Does:

  • Give you a ready-to-deploy JSON and CSV for the interactive
  • Provide proper attribution for every data point
  • Give you ready language for captions, tooltips, and the cover letter
  • Flag what’s solid (BBC-reported) vs what’s adjacent (AP-reported)

Doesn’t:

  • Replace primary research (it is compiled from news reports, not underlying market data)
  • Include tick-level price series (you’d need TradingView/Yahoo Finance if you want to build actual price charts inside the detail view)
  • Claim anything the BBC didn’t claim (important for defensibility)

If you want tick-level price data for a detail view:

  • S&P 500 (SPY) intraday for 9 April 2025: available on Yahoo Finance historical data, free
  • Oil futures (WTI / Brent) intraday for 9 March and 23 March 2026: Yahoo Finance or TradingView, free tier sufficient
  • This would add ~2 hours to your Day 4 but would make the detail views substantially richer. Optional.
